Humanoid projectile dodging — PPO on CPU MuJoCo

The idea (as agreed in session) A physically simulated human figure with full-body control learns to dodge slow-ish projectiles. It must react, not memorize — every spawn is randomized. Joint anatomy is respected. It lives in a 3×3×3 m box: the floor is safe, the sides kill, projectiles phase through the walls. Spawns are far away (8–15 m) so there is time to adapt; aim is computed once at launch — some shots precise, some offset, targeting whatever body pose it's in (prone → shot from above).

01

Design invariants (user-set, binding)

These override implementation convenience. A task that can't satisfy one returns BLOCKED rather than bending it.

Reactive, not memorized
Every projectile's spawn position, direction, speed, timing and target link come from the env's seeded RNG, fresh each episode. No fixed scenario exists anywhere in the code.
Joint anatomy respected
Stock Gymnasium humanoid joint range values used unchanged (knee −160°…−2°, elbow −90°…50°, …). Never widened, never removed; guarded by tests in Tasks 1 and 2.
3×3×3 arena — sides kill, floor doesn't
Torso |x|>1.5 m or |y|>1.5 m ⇒ −100 and termination. No wall geometry at all — a position check. Falling is survivable; getting up is part of the task.
Projectiles: far, straight, launch-aimed
Spawn 8–15 m out on the full shell (incl. overhead), fly straight at constant speed (gravity-compensated), aim computed once at launch — no homing. They contact only humanoid geoms: floor, walls and each other are pass-through. Despawn on hit, TTL, or >20 m.
Pose-targeted aiming
Target is a sampled body link at its current position — a prone body draws fire from above, so no static pose is safe. Coin-flip per shot: precise at link centre vs Gaussian offset (σ = 0.35 m).

Amendment (2026-08-03, supersedes the table above as originally written): the reward is now survival time and nothing else. The upright bonus, proximity shaping and control cost are gone, as are the −50 hit and −100 wall penalties. A shorter episode is the entire punishment, so no term can trade against survival or be farmed independently of it. This also reverses the earlier "hit is non-terminal; wall is the only death" decision — any projectile contact now ends the episode. min_approach survives as diagnostics only. Golden hash moved a55500a9… → e70208b8….

Observation: humanoid qpos[2:] + qvel (projectile joints masked out) + wall block [1.5−x, 1.5+x, 1.5−y, 1.5+y] + 4 slots × [active, rel_pos·3, rel_vel·3], sorted by time-to-closest-approach — memorization impossible, reaction sufficient.

Amendment (2026-08-03): the wall block was added after the first training run showed 24–33% of episodes ending in wall death — the arena is the only terminal failure mode, and excluding root x,y made it invisible to the policy. Signed per-wall distances rather than the originally-sketched 1.5−|x|: the absolute-value form is symmetric in x, so it conveys how near the wall is but not which way to run. Each entry is positive inside the arena and crosses zero exactly at termination. Observation width 73 → 77; golden hash deliberately moved cd3999d6… → a55500a9…. The change is not resumable — it widens the policy input layer, so the 713,538-step run ended here.

Why it works, and why the bench under-predicted it: SubprocVecEnv steps every worker in lockstep and then runs ONE policy forward over the batch, so at --n-envs 6 the box sat at ~2.2 busy cores out of 12 — lockstep-bound, not CPU-bound. On the clean 4-vCPU runner the same change measured only 1.35×, because that machine was already core-saturated; the box had ~10 idle cores sitting behind the stall. Direction transfers between machines, magnitude does not. Separately: the box runs everything ~5.7× slower than a clean runner (its test suite takes 238s vs 41s), so the originally-assumed ~1,600 steps/s target was never reachable there.
Learning dynamics are unchanged: gradient updates for a fixed step budget are total_steps × n_epochs / batch_size, independent of n_envs, and n_steps=2048 per env keeps the GAE horizon identical. Wider batches, same update cadence.

06

Evidence this fits the box

Measured here (2026-08-03, under live load): Humanoid-v5 1,655 env-steps/s single process; 8,856/s across 6 processes; PPO 2×256 update 18–24 k samples/s → all-in ~1.8–3.6 k env-steps/s ⇒ 10 M steps ≈ 1–1.5 h, 100 M ≈ 8–15 h.
PAC-MAN (Yang, Li, Ames · arXiv:2607.28623) — humanoid dodges thrown balls with PPO; closest-approach evasion + upright reward + fall penalty; no curriculum needed; validates the exact observation design.
Step budgets: tuned PPO walks Humanoid-v4 in 10 M steps (rl-baselines3-zoo); SAC reaches 5,265 return at 1 M (CleanRL benchmarks) but is gradient-bound on this CPU — PPO is the route.
MuJoCo CPU throughput: 27-DoF humanoid ~30 k physics-steps/s per modern core (mujoco#986) — physics is not the bottleneck; the Python/learner loop is, and it's measured above.
